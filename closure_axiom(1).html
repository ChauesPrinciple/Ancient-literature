Sacred Mathematics

Three traditions, across two millennia, converged on the same deep structure. The śulba sūtras (Vedic India, 800–500 BCE) used ropes stretched across fire altars to prove the Pythagorean theorem — centuries before Pythagoras. Al-Bīrūnī (Islamic Persia, 973–1048 CE) extended flat geometry to the sphere to calculate the qibla, the direction of Mecca from any point on Earth. Dante Alighieri (1265–1321 CE) encoded this same spherical cosmos into The Divine Comedy, where Earth and God are antipodal points on a 3-sphere — finite, boundaryless, with no outside.

Chronological and geometric progression below: The triangle is the foundation of all sacred measurement. From there: the circle (S¹), the sphere (S²), spherical trigonometry, and finally the 3-sphere (S³) containing Dante's entire cosmos.

Key insight: When Dante and Virgil descend Satan's body at Earth's center and "down becomes up," this is antipodal geometry — the same closure property that defines the 3-sphere. The sacred cannot leak because there is no outside.
